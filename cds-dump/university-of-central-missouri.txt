72:Common Data Set 2025-2026
75:Welcome to the 2025-2026 Common Data Set collection!
72:The Common Data Set (CDS) initiative is a collaborative effort among data providers in the higher
72:education community and publishers as represented by the College Board, Peterson’s, and U.S. News &
72:World Report. The combined goal of this collaboration is to improve the quality and accuracy of
72:information provided to all involved in a student’s transition into higher education, as well as to reduce
72:the reporting burden on data providers.
74:The CDS is a set of standards and definitions of data items rather than a survey instrument or set of data
72:represented in a database. Each of the higher education surveys conducted by the participating publishers
72:incorporates items from the CDS as well as unique items proprietary to each publisher. Consequently, the
72:publishers’ surveys differ in that they utilize varying numbers of items from the CDS.
74:Those who report data for their colleges are urged to abide by the definitions and the cohorts specified
72:when answering CDS items. They are also urged to use the answers to CDS items when responding to the
72:numerous survey requests they receive, by distributing photocopies of their answers, posting them on
72:their websites, or by other effective means.
72:This Excel template is designed for CSV export. On each section tab, responses are stored in columns AA–
72:AL. Before exporting, verify that your entries appear correctly on the Answer Sheet. The Answer Sheet
72:includes field mappings to the U.S. News fillable PDF and to the Word version. Please confirm with the
72:receiving organization that it is able to accept a CSV of your responses. For more information about the
72:Common Data Set (CDS) initiative, visit https://commondataset.org/
74:Please contact the College Board College Survey team at collegesurvey@collegeboard.org should you
72:have any questions about this Excel template.
72:*Note: At the time of publication, not all questions on the Excel template can be matched to a U.S. News
72:fillable PDF equivalent. The matching cells for these questions are in light red on the Answer Sheet. Please
72:confirm all questions, responses, and mapping before submitting a CSV to another organization.
72:*Note: As of publication, some questions in the Word template do not map directly—or require different
72:formatting—to an equivalent field in the U.S. News fillable PDF and in the Excel template. An example are
72:those questions with multiple category options such as "Very Important, Important, Considered, Not
72:Considered". The matching cells for these questions may appear as duplicates (light red on the Answer
72:Sheet) or a single category is listed as the code value (c7_state_residency_very_important ). Please review
72:and verify all questions, responses, and mapping requirements before exporting to CSV or submitting to
72:another organization.

72:institution's own standards.
72:Need-based aid: College-funded or college-administered award from institutional, state, federal, or
72:other sources for which a student must have financial need to qualify. This includes both institutional
72:and non-institutional student aid (grants, jobs, and loans).
72:Need-based scholarship or grant aid: Scholarships and grants from institutional, state, federal, or other
72:sources for which a student must have financial need to qualify.
72:Need-based self-help aid: Loans and jobs from institutional, state, federal, or other sources for which a
72:student must demonstrate financial need to qualify.
72:Non-need-based scholarship or grant aid: Scholarships and grants, gifts, or merit-based aid from
72:institutional, state, federal, or other sources (including unrestricted funds or gifts and endowment
72:income) awarded solely on the basis of academic achievement, merit, or any other non-need-based

224:Financial Aid Definitions
72:External scholarships and grants: Scholarships and grants received from outside (private) sources that
72:students bring with them (e.g., Kiwanis, National Merit scholarships). The institution may process
72:paperwork to receive the dollars, but it has no role in determining the recipient or the dollar amount
72:awarded.
72:Financial aid applicant: Any applicant who submits any one of the institutionally required financial aid
72:applications/forms, such as the FAFSA.
72:Indebtedness: Aggregate dollar amount borrowed through any loan program (federal, state, subsidized,
72:unsubsidized, private, etc.; excluding parent loans) while the student was enrolled at an institution.
72:Student loans co-signed by a parent are assumed to be the responsibility of the student and should be
72:included.
72:Institutional scholarships and grants: Endowed scholarships, annual gifts and tuition funded grants for
72:which the institution determines the recipient.
72:Financial need: As determined by your institution using the federal methodology and/or your institution's
72:own standards.
72:Need-based aid: College-funded or college-administered award from institutional, state, federal, or other
72:sources for which a student must have financial need to qualify. This includes both institutional and non-
72:institutional student aid (grants, jobs, and loans).
72:Need-based scholarship or grant aid: Scholarships and grants from institutional, state, federal, or other
72:sources for which a student must have financial need to qualify.
72:Need-based self-help aid: Loans and jobs from institutional, state, federal, or other sources for which a
72:student must demonstrate financial need to qualify.
72:Non-need-based scholarship or grant aid: Scholarships and grants, gifts, or merit-based aid from
72:institutional, state, federal, or other sources (including unrestricted funds or gifts and endowment
72:income) awarded solely on the basis of academic achievement, merit, or any other non-need-based
72:reason. When reporting questions H1 and H2, non-need-based aid that is used to meet need should be
72:counted as need-based aid.

72:student need not demonstrate financial need to qualify.
