86:Common Data Set 2025-2026
86:Welcome to the 2025-2026 Common Data Set collection!
86:The | 101:Common Data Set (CDS) | 193:initiative is a collaborative effort among data providers in the higher education community
86:and publishers as represented by the | 215:College Board | 267:, | 271:Peterson’s | 311:, and | 330:U.S. News & World Report | 426:. The combined goal
86:of this collaboration is to improve the quality and accuracy of information provided to all involved in a student’s transition
86:into higher education, as well as to reduce the reporting burden on data providers.
86:The CDS is a set of standards and definitions of data items rather than a survey instrument or set of data represented in
86:a database. Each of the higher education surveys conducted by the participating publishers incorporates items from the
86:CDS as well as unique items proprietary to each publisher. Consequently, the publishers’ surveys differ in that they utilize
86:varying numbers of items from the CDS.
86:Those who report data for their colleges are urged to abide by the definitions and the cohorts specified when answering
86:CDS items. They are also urged to use the answers to CDS items when responding to the numerous survey requests
86:they receive, by distributing photocopies of their answers, posting them on their websites, or by other effective means.
86:This Excel template is designed for CSV export. On each section tab, responses are stored in columns | 437:AA–AL | 463:. Before
86:exporting, verify that your entries appear correctly on the | 281:Answer Sheet | 332:. The Answer Sheet includes field mappings to
86:the | 99:U.S. News fillable PDF | 182:and to the | 219:Word | 240:version. Please confirm with the receiving organization that it is able to
86:accept a CSV of your responses. For more information about the Common Data Set (CDS) initiative, visit
86:https://commondataset.org/
86:Please contact the | 151:College Board College Survey | 264:team at collegesurvey@collegeboard.org should you have any
86:questions about this Excel template.
86:*Note: At the time of publication, not all questions on the | 279:Excel | 302:template can be matched to a | 405:U.S. News fillable PDF
86:equivalent. The matching cells for these questions are in light red on the Answer Sheet. Please confirm all questions,
86:responses, and mapping before submitting a CSV to another organization.
86:*Note: As of publication, some questions in the | 247:Word | 269:template do not map directly—or require different formatting—to an
86:equivalent field in the | 160:U.S. News fillable PDF | 244:and in the | 280:Excel | 302:template. An example are those questions with multiple
86:category options such as "Very Important, Important, Considered, Not Considered". The matching cells for these
86:questions may appear as duplicates (light red on the Answer Sheet) or a single category is listed as the code value
86:(c7_state_residency_very_important ). Please review and verify all questions, responses, and mapping requirements
86:before exporting to CSV or submitting to another organization.

56:Financial need: | 106:As determined by your institution using the federal methodology and/or your institution's own standards.
56:Need-based aid: | 108:College-funded or college-administered award from institutional, state, federal, or other sources for which a student must have financial
56:need to qualify. This includes both institutional and non-institutional student aid (grants, jobs, and loans).
56:Need-based scholarship or grant aid: | 173:Scholarships and grants from institutional, state, federal, or other sources for which a student must have financial
56:need to qualify.
56:Need-based self-help aid: | 136:Loans and jobs from institutional, state, federal, or other sources for which a student must demonstrate financial need to
56:qualify.
56:Non-need-based scholarship or grant aid: | 187:Scholarships and grants, gifts, or merit-based aid from institutional, state, federal, or other sources (including
56:unrestricted funds or gifts and endowment income) awarded solely on the basis of academic achievement, merit, or any other non-need-based reason.
56:When reporting questions H1 and H2, non-need-based aid that is used to meet need should be counted as need-based aid.
56:Note: Suggested order of precedence for counting non-need money as need-based:
56:1. | 73:Non-need institutional grants
56:2. | 73:Non-need tuition waivers
56:3. | 73:Non-need athletic awards
56:4. | 73:Non-need federal grants
56:5. | 73:Non-need state grants
56:6. | 73:Non-need outside grants
56:7. | 73:Non-need student loans
56:8. | 73:Non-need parent loans
56:9. | 73:Non-need work
56:Non-need-based self-help aid: | 150:Loans and jobs from institutional, state, or other sources for which a student need not demonstrate financial need to
56:qualify.
56:Work study and employment | 146:: Federal and state work study aid, and any employment packaged by your institution in financial aid awards.
