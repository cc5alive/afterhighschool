38:Cooperative education program: | 134:A program that provides for alternate class attendance and employment in business, industry, or
38:government.
38:Cooperative housing: | 101:College-owned, -operated, or -affiliated housing in which students share food and housing expenses and participate
38:in household chores to reduce living expenses.
38:*Counseling service: | 99:Activities designed to assist students in making plans and decisions related to their education, career, or personal
38:development.
38:Credit: | 60:Recognition of attendance or performance in an instructional activity (course or program) that can be applied by a recipient
38:toward the requirements for a degree, diploma, certificate, or recognized postsecondary credential.
38:Credit course: | 81:A course that, if successfully completed, can be applied toward the number of courses required for achieving a degree,
38:diploma, certificate, or other recognized postsecondary credential.
38:Credit hour: | 76:A unit of measure representing an hour (50 minutes) of instruction over a 15-week period in a semester or trimester system
38:or a 10-week period in a quarter system. It is applied toward the total number of hours needed for completing the requirements of a
38:degree, diploma, certificate, or recognized postsecondary credential.
38:Cross-registration: | 94:A system whereby students enrolled at one institution may take courses at another institution without having to apply
38:to the second institution.
38:Deferred admission: | 98:The practice of permitting admitted students to postpone enrollment, usually for a period of one academic term or
38:one year.
38:Degree: | 62:An award conferred by a college, university, or other postsecondary education institution as official recognition for the successful
38:completion of a program of studies.
38:Degree-seeking students: | 111:Students enrolled in courses for credit who are recognized by the institution as seeking a degree or recognized
38:postsecondary credential. At the undergraduate level, this is intended to include students enrolled in vocational or occupational programs.
38:*Developmental services: | 112:Instructional courses designed for students deficient in the general competencies necessary for a regular
38:postsecondary curriculum and educational setting.
38:Differs by program (calendar system): | 150:A calendar system classification that is used by institutions that have occupational/vocational
38:programs of varying length. These schools may enroll students at specific times depending on the program desired. For example, a school
38:might offer a two-month program in January, March, May, September, and November; and a three-month program in January, April, and
38:October.
38:Diploma: | 66:See | 78:Postsecondary award, certificate, or diploma.
38:Distance learning: | 92:An option for earning course credit at off-campus locations via cable television, internet, satellite classes, videotapes,
38:correspondence courses, or other means.
38:Doctor’s degree-research/scholarship | 145:: A Ph.D. or other doctor's degree that requires advanced work beyond the master’s level,
38:including the preparation and defense of a dissertation based on original research, or the planning and execution of an original project
38:demonstrating substantial artistic or scholarly achievement. Some examples of this type of degree may include Ed.D., D.M.A., D.B.A.,
38:D.Sc., D.A., or D.M, and others, as designated by the awarding institution.
38:Doctor’s degree-professional practice | 145:: A doctor’s degree that is conferred upon completion of a program providing the knowledge and
38:skills for the recognition, credential, or license required for professional practice. The degree is awarded after a period of study such that
38:the total time to the degree, including both pre-professional and professional preparation, equals at least six full-time equivalent academic
38:years. Some of these degrees were formerly classified as “first-professional” and may include: Chiropractic (D.C. or D.C.M.); Dentistry
38:(D.D.S. or D.M.D.); Law (L.L.B. or J.D.); Medicine (M.D.); Optometry (O.D.); Osteopathic Medicine (D.O); Pharmacy (Pharm.D.);
38:Podiatry (D.P.M., Pod.D., D.P.); or, Veterinary Medicine (D.V.M.), and others, as designated by the awarding institution.
38:Doctor’s degree-other | 101:: A doctor’s degree that does not meet the definition of a doctor’s degree - research/scholarship or a doctor’s
38:degree - professional practice.
38:Double major: | 81:Program in which students may complete two undergraduate programs of study simultaneously.
38:Dual enrollment: | 89:Refers to high school students enrolled in college courses for credit. In accordance with IPEDS, student performance is
38:recorded on a college transcript and postsecondary credit is awarded for a passing grade in the course. Dual enrollment includes: All
38:postsecondary courses, independent of course delivery mode, course location, course instructor, whether secondary credit is also offered,
38:and whether the student enrolls through a formal state/local program or enrolls outside a formal state/local program. Dual enrollment
38:excludes: Credit-by-exam models such as Advanced Placement (AP) and International Baccalaureate (IB) in which the student is not
38:enrolled in a postsecondary institution.
38:Early action plan: | 91:An admission plan that allows students to apply and be notified of an admission decision well in advance of the regular
38:notification dates. If admitted, the candidate is not committed to enroll; the student may reply to the offer under the college’s regular reply
38:policy.
38:Early admission: | 88:A policy under which students who have not completed high school are admitted and enroll full time in college, usually
38:after completion of their junior year.
38:Early decision plan: | 97:A plan that permits students to apply and be notified of an admission decision (and financial aid offer if applicable)
38:well in advance of the regular notification date. Applicants agree to accept an offer of admission and, if admitted, to withdraw their
38:applications from other colleges. There are three possible decisions for early decision applicants: admitted, denied, or not admitted but
38:forwarded for consideration with the regular applicant pool, without prejudice.
38:English as a Second Language (ESL): | 147:A course of study designed specifically for students whose native language is not English.
38:Exchange student program-domestic: | 148:Any arrangement between a student and a college that permits study for a semester or more at
38:another college | 80:in the United States | 138:without extending the amount of time required for a degree. | 301:See also Study abroad | 364:.
38:External degree program: | 115:A program of study in which students earn credits toward a degree through independent study, college
38:courses, proficiency examinations, and personal experience. External degree programs require minimal or no classroom attendance.

38:Extracurricular activities (as admission factor): | 177:Special consideration in the admissions process given for participation in both school
38:and nonschool-related activities of interest to the college, such as clubs, hobbies, student government, athletics, performing arts, etc.
38:First-time student: | 94:A student attending any institution for the first time at the level enrolled. Includes students enrolled in the fall term
38:who attended a postsecondary institution for the first time at the same level in the prior summer term. Also includes students who entered
38:with advanced standing (college credit earned before graduation from high school).
38:First-time, first-year student: | 124:A student attending any institution for the first time at the undergraduate level. Includes students enrolled
38:in the fall term who attended college for the first time in the prior summer term. Also includes students who entered with advanced
38:standing (college credits earned before graduation from high school).
38:First-year student: | 93:A student who has completed less than the equivalent of 1 full year of undergraduate work; that is, less than 30
38:semester hours (in a 120-hour degree program) or less than 900 clock hours.
38:Food and housing (charges)—on campus: | 159:Assume double occupancy in institutional housing and 19 meals per week (or maximum meal
38:plan).
38:Full-time student (undergraduate): | 141:A student enrolled for 12 or more semester credits, 12 or more quarter credits, or 24 or more clock
38:hours a week each term.
38:Geographical residence (as admission factor): | 171:Special consideration in the admission process given to students from a particular region,
38:state, or country of residence.
38:Grade-point average (academic high school GPA): | 184:The sum of grade points a student has earned in secondary school divided by the
38:number of courses taken. The most common system of assigning numbers to grades counts four points for an A, three points for a B, two
38:points for a C, one point for a D, and no points for an E or F. Unweighted GPA’s assign the same weight to each course. Weighting gives
38:students additional points for their grades in advanced or honors courses.
38:Graduate student: | 92:A student who holds a bachelor’s or equivalent, and is taking courses at the post-baccalaureate level.
38:*Health services: | 89:Free or low cost on-campus primary and preventive health care available to students.
38:High school diploma or recognized equivalent: | 173:A document certifying the successful completion of a prescribed secondary school
38:program of studies, or the attainment of satisfactory scores on the Tests of General Educational Development (GED), or another state-
38:specified examination.
38:Hispanic or Latino: | 97:A person of Mexican, Puerto Rican, Cuban, South or Central American, or other Spanish culture or origin,
38:regardless of race.
38:Honors program: | 90:Any special program for very able students offering the opportunity for educational enrichment, independent study,
38:acceleration, or some combination of these.
38:Independent study: | 95:Academic work chosen or designed by the student with the approval of the department concerned, under an
38:instructor’s supervision, and usually undertaken outside of the regular classroom structure.
38:In-state tuition: | 85:The tuition charged by institutions to those students who meet the state’s or institution’s residency requirements.
38:International student: | 103:See | 114:Nonresident.
38:International student group: | 122:Student groups that facilitate cultural dialogue, support a diverse campus, assist international students in
38:acclimation and creating a social network.
38:Internship: | 72:Any short-term, supervised work experience usually related to a student’s major field, for which the student earns academic
38:credit. The work can be full- or part-time, on- or off-campus, paid or unpaid.
38:*Learning center: | 91:Center offering assistance through tutors, workshops, computer programs, or audiovisual equipment in reading,
38:writing, math, and skills such as taking notes, managing time, taking tests.
38:*Legal services: | 85:Free or low cost legal advice for a range of issues (personal and other).
38:Liberal arts/career combination: | 134:Program in which a student earns undergraduate degrees in two separate fields, one in a liberal arts
38:major and the other in a professional or specialized major, whether on campus or through cross‑registration.
38:Living learning community: | 120:Residential programs that allow students to interact with students who share common interests. In addition
38:to living together, students may also participate in shared courses, special events, and group service projects.
38:Master's degree | 84:: An award that requires the successful completion of a program of study of generally one or two full-time equivalent
38:academic years of work beyond the bachelor's degree. Some of these degrees, such as those in Theology (M.Div., M.H.L./Rav) that were
38:formerly classified as "first-professional", may require more than two full-time equivalent academic years of work.
38:Minority affiliation (as admission factor): | 159:Special consideration in the admission process for members of designated racial/ethnic
38:minority groups.
38:*Minority student center: | 114:Center with programs, activities, and/or services intended to enhance the college experience of students of
38:color.
38:Model United Nations: | 105:A simulation activity focusing on conflict resolution, globalization, and diplomacy. Assuming roles as foreign
38:ambassadors and “delegates,” students conduct research, engage in debate, draft resolutions, and may participate in a national Model UN
38:conference.
38:Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander: | 162:A person having origins in any of the original peoples of Hawaii, Guam, Samoa, or
38:other Pacific Islands.
38:*New student orientation: | 114:Orientation addressing the academic, social, emotional, and intellectual issues involved in beginning college.
38:May be a few hours or a few days in length; at some colleges, there is a fee.
38:Nonresident: | 77:A person who is not a citizen or national of the United States and who is in this country on a visa or temporary basis and
38:does not have the right to remain indefinitely.
38:*On-campus day care: | 104:Licensed day care for students’ children (usually age 3 and up); usually for a fee.

38:Post-baccalaureate certificate: | 127:An award that requires completion of an organized program of study requiring 18 credit hours beyond
38:the bachelor’s; designed for persons who have completed a baccalaureate degree but do not meet the requirements of academic degrees
38:carrying the title of master.

38:Less Than 1 Academic Year | 117:: | 122:Requires completion of an organized program of study at the postsecondary level (below the baccalaureate

38:Private institution: | 94:An educational institution controlled by a private individual(s) or by a nongovernmental agency, usually supported
38:primarily by other than public funds, and operated by other than publicly elected or appointed officials.
38:Private for-profit institution: | 123:A private institution in which the individual(s) or agency in control receives compensation, other than
38:wages, rent, or other expenses for the assumption of risk.
38:Private nonprofit institution: | 123:A private institution in which the individual(s) or agency in control receives no compensation, other than
38:wages, rent, or other expenses for the assumption of risk. These include both independent nonprofit schools and those affiliated with a

38:Public institution: | 91:An educational institution whose programs and activities are operated by publicly elected or appointed school
38:officials, and which is supported primarily by public funds.
38:Quarter calendar system: | 113:A calendar system in which the academic year consists of three sessions called quarters of about 12 weeks
38:each. The range may be from 10 to 15 weeks. There may be an additional quarter in the summer.
38:Race/ethnicity: | 83:Category used to describe groups to which individuals belong, identify with, or belong in the eyes of the community. The
38:categories do not denote scientific definitions of anthropological origins. A person may be counted in only one group.
38:Race/ethnicity unknown: | 111:Category used to classify students or employees whose race/ethnicity is not known and whom institutions are
38:unable to place in one of the specified racial/ethnic categories.

38:Secondary school record (as admission factor): | 174:Information maintained by the secondary school that may include such things as the
38:student’s high school transcript, class rank, GPA, and teacher and counselor recommendations.
